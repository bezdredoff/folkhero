## Record standard
Record source ID, author/institution, original date and publication date, region, social setting, object/page/section, access status, supported claim, excluded inference and affected concepts.
REVIEWED means the named accessible text was inspected; CATALOGUE-ONLY means metadata, not contents. SEARCH-EXCERPT means full-page access failed. None means design approved.
External images require their own rights/attribution check before redistribution; links alone do not authorize republishing.

## R01 — rural GDL life (ARCH/CLOTH/TOOLS)
Neringa Dambrauskaitė, “Lietuvos Didžiosios Kunigaikštystės valstiečių kasdienio gyvenimo detalės XVI amžiuje–XVII amžiaus pirmoje pusėje”, Lietuvos istorijos studijos 46 (2020), pp.25–42. DOI 10.15388/LIS.2020.46.2.
URL: https://www.zurnalai.vu.lt/lietuvos-istorijos-studijos/en/article/download/5031/21419
Access: REVIEWED, scholarly original research based on court records and inventories; P2 synthesis with cited P1 documents.
Scope: peasants on private noble estates, GDL; individual examples require their own dates/locations.
Findings: homesteads combine dwelling, storage and livestock buildings; richer ones have more. Smoke-heated dwellings and vestibules occur; Samogitian numas is geographically specific. Clothing includes linen shirts, wool outer garments, sheepskins and women's wraps. The Apranga section reports a 1582 Minsk-district household with black outer garments, a blue wrap and boots; this is a wealthier household, not a universal costume.
Use: B01–B06, F02/F03, G01/G02, tool research.
Limits: no measured Upper Berezina reconstruction or complete garment cutting pattern. Separate later examples from chosen game date. Follow cited primary records for disputed details.

## R02 — regional architectural scholarship (ARCH)
Yu. A. Yakimovich, Zodchestvo Belorussii XVI–serediny XVII v., Minsk: Navuka i tekhnika, 1991; ISBN 5-343-00466-0.
URL: https://books.totalarch.com/architecture_of_belarus_16_17_century
Access: REVIEWED available excerpt on third-party host, not entire book; P2.
Locator: housing discussion beginning “Простейший тип жилья”.
Findings: rectangular/square timber rooms, pitched roofs with straw or split-wood coverings, smoke-heated and chimney-heated forms, and vestibule combinations occur in the discussed corpus.
Limits: the nearby passage includes estate/castle servants and broader chronology; it does not prove every option for an ordinary Upper Berezina household. Check original inventory/date before selecting details.
Use: B01/B02 construction alternatives; measured roof pitch, exact opening layout and dimensions remain assumptions until sourced.

## R08 — local folklore and narrative divergence (SPIRIT)
N. Ya. Nikiforovsky, Nechistiki, Vilna 1907, section XXI “Болотники, багники, оржавеники, лозники”, pp.80–85.
URL: https://ru.wikisource.org/wiki/Нечистики_(Никифоровский)/Болотники/ДО
Access: REVIEWED transcription; this page is tagged as lacking scans, so original scan verification remains.
Classification: E; Vitebsk Belarus folklore recorded centuries after game time.
Findings: swamp beings are attached to particular wet habitats; the text's Bolotnik is heavy, eyeless and mud-covered, lures victims and depends on wet conditions.
Design implications (game interpretation): broad low silhouette and habitat-linked surface cues are candidates; individual tiny creatures on the body can be omitted under the detail budget.
Divergence: this account portrays a predatory being, not a neutral contractual guardian. Folkhero's pact, territorial neutrality, visible mobile boss and boss-safe status responses are deliberate fiction. Do not present these as the source's description.
Use: I01–I03/E03; no automatic lightning immunity despite the folklore account, because locked gameplay requires boss participation in reactions.
