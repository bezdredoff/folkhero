# Game Vision

Status: v0.2 — design direction locked enough for pre-production. Exact values remain subject to prototype and playtest validation.

## One-sentence direction

**Folkhero is a story-driven cooperative systemic action RPG / hack-and-slash set in an alternate-history late Grand Duchy of Lithuania / early Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth world, where Belarusian and wider Slavic folklore is expressed through reusable gameplay systems, weather, environments, enemies, rituals, choices and consequences.**

The game is **not primarily a roguelite**. Character progression is persistent and Diablo-like: levels, equipment, skills and learned ornaments carry forward.

## Core ambition

Create a game that feels larger than its team because a small number of reusable systems combine into many situations.

The target is not a huge handcrafted catalogue. It is a strong interaction grammar:
- abilities are composed from reusable primitives;
- enemies reuse chassis, behaviors and capability packages;
- statuses interact with each other and with surfaces;
- weather and seasons materially alter combat;
- ornaments reshape builds and skill behavior;
- world-state variables make choices visible in old content;
- modular regions can be revisited under different systemic conditions.

The project is developed as two products at once:
1. the game;
2. the content factory that makes future content cheaper to produce.

## Product pillars

### 1. Hack-and-slash first, RPG second

Moment-to-moment combat must remain readable, responsive and satisfying.

Narrative, progression and systemic depth should enrich combat rather than bury it under long interruptions or complex menu work.

### 2. Systems over bespoke mechanics

New content should normally be expressed through reusable primitives, data, tags, modifiers and composition.

A new mechanic is most valuable when it can support several of:
- player classes;
- enemies;
- bosses;
- loot;
- environments;
- quests;
- future content.

### 3. Folklore as gameplay rules

Folklore is not only a visual theme.

Spirits, rituals, taboos, boundaries and old agreements should define mechanics and world rules.

Many local spirits are neutral or territorial rather than inherently evil. They follow rules, contracts and boundaries. Humans can create danger by breaking those rules.

The setting also contains genuinely malicious spirits, curses, undead, corruption and other hostile supernatural forces.

### 4. Persistent build experimentation

Weapons are platforms, not disposable stat sticks.

Players should care more about:
- weapon behavior;
- ornaments;
- status interactions;
- systemic synergies;
- skill variants;
- situational strengths;

than about replacing equipment every few minutes for a slightly larger damage number.

### 5. Weather, seasons and surfaces are gameplay

Rain, flooding, snow, ice, heat, blizzards and fog should materially affect combat and traversal.

The same region should play differently under different seasonal and weather states.

### 6. Systemic consequences

Choices should update a persistent world state rather than explode into many bespoke campaign branches.

Consequences may alter:
- patrols;
- route safety;
- NPC and merchant availability;
- enemy mixes;
- events;
- visuals;
- side quests;
- environmental conditions;
- final campaign state.

### 7. Small team, production multiplier

Art, gameplay and world production should be designed for:
- modular reuse;
- shared rigs;
- shared materials;
- procedural assembly;
- AI-assisted production;
- automated validation;
- strong human approval gates.

A successful pipeline makes the second and third similar content items cheaper than the first.

## Setting direction

Historical skeleton:
- late 16th century;
- late Grand Duchy of Lithuania / early Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth;
- Livonian War used as a major historical frame;
- real states, places, rulers and events may appear.

This is alternate history, not strict reconstruction.

The supernatural is real and may significantly alter events.

Major historical rulers should usually influence the player through:
- decrees;
- campaigns;
- envoys;
- rumors;
- letters;
- military policy;

rather than long face-to-face political dialogue sequences.

## Core narrative theme

**Home, displacement and belonging.**

The heroes' home is not destroyed. It becomes politically unsafe and inaccessible.

The player characters resist a new authority when it destroys a ritual site and breaks an old local pact. They become fugitives and are forced to leave home.

A familiar displaced village community remains an emotional and functional anchor through the campaign.

The long-term payoff is a safe return to a changed home.

Two major end-state routes are planned:
1. gain protection/patronage from an influential side that can guarantee safe return;
2. reach a politically gray outcome where war or a change in power makes return possible without a clean exoneration.

## Campaign shape

Target for the first full game:
- 5 narrative acts;
- 3 major reusable regions;
- approximately 8–10 hours of mandatory main story;
- approximately 12–15 hours for a typical first playthrough with side content;
- roughly 3–4 authored main quests per act;
- repeatable farming/exploration in dungeons and dangerous wilderness.

Main quest structure:
**fixed story + variable journey**.

Fixed:
- key location;
- important NPC;
- narrative beat;
- core outcome.

Variable:
- route/layout;
- weather;
- time;
- encounters;
- optional objectives;
- world-state modifiers;
- loot.

## Player characters

Initial scope is deliberately limited to two foundational archetypes:

### Warrior

A melee/cold-weapon foundation.

Initial weapon families:
- axe;
- spear.

Physical damage must remain fully viable without requiring an elemental build.

### Vedun

A ranged/folk-magic foundation.

Initial combat shape:
- direct projectile spell;
- persistent area/ritual/zone spell.

Both classes use the same systemic status grammar differently.

Future classes may specialize more heavily in one affinity or support several primary affinities, but that is outside current scope.

## Platform direction

- PC is the lead development and Early Access platform.
- Architecture, UI, controls and performance must remain mobile-friendly from the start.
- iOS/Android are later release targets, after core gameplay, networking and performance are proven.
- Camera is 3D top-down/isometric with fixed or very limited rotation and a controlled zoom range.

## Multiplayer

Co-op is a committed core feature.

Long-term target: 1–4 players.

Pre-production, Vertical Slice and first Early Access validation prioritize:
- solo;
- 2-player co-op.

Guest players keep character progression and loot. Narrative/world-state progression belongs to the host campaign.

## Early Access direction

The first Early Access release does not need several regions.

Target:
- 1 complete region;
- 1 complete first act;
- about 2–3 hours of main story;
- additional side content, farming and replayable procedural areas;
- meaningful seasonal/weather variants;
- both base classes;
- co-op;
- core ornament/progression/status systems.

The Early Access build should prove the content-reuse model rather than impress through raw biome count.

## Business model

Premium buy-to-play.

Core progression power is not sold.

Allowed future monetization may include:
- substantial paid expansions;
- optional cosmetic DLC.

Do not sell:
- power;
- ornaments;
- loot;
- energy;
- progression acceleration.

## Success test

The game is moving in the right direction if:
- simple combat already feels good with primitive art;
- the same status systems matter for players, enemies, bosses and environments;
- a region feels meaningfully different under new weather/season/world states;
- a different ornament build changes how the same skills are used;
- co-op creates systemic combinations instead of only doubling damage;
- the same content-production grammar makes each related content item cheaper to build;
- players want to replay familiar content because its systems combine differently.
