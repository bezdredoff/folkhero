## Population
Local Belarusian/Ruthenian households are a substantial majority and set the village's everyday character. Working target 80–90% of permanent residents; this is an art/narrative target, not a historical census claim.
Cossack experience is a biography/social-military affiliation, not an ethnic census category; it can overlap with a local resident.
Several minority families live here permanently; no equal-sized groups or ethnic costume parade.
Hair/skin/facial features vary within groups. Clothing, family history, personal objects, names and dialogue carry identity; no exclusive ethnic body/face rig.
Tatar category here is Tatars of the GDL (later often called Lipka), not a generic “Tatar-Mongol” army.
The approved village plan has exactly 23 households: HH-01–HH-09 plus 14 additional local farming households HH-10–HH-23. HH-01 is separate and must not be counted twice. Named-family headcounts and local/minority resident share still need census reconciliation; household counts do not guarantee 80–90%. See [accepted plan](../Art/Act_I_Village_Plan.md).

## Weaver household — exact family continuity
Seven residents before departure: six women/girls and one man.
- Mother: established local weaver.
- Father/husband.
- Eldest daughter: independently capable textile/ornament worker, joins heroes.
- Two younger daughters.
- Mother's elder sister (the leaving daughter's aunt).
- Grandmother; exact maternal/paternal branch and age not locked.
Six stay home. The kinship interpretation “mother's elder sister” was explicitly accepted in discussion.
Allocate distinct skills and personalities later, not seven bespoke unrelated costume systems.
Mother is not replaced by daughter at home: the daughter's portable service supports the displaced hub while the mother continues in the village.
No exact age/romance/backstory invented for the eldest daughter.

## Ordinary work
Farming households provide field/garden work, livestock, hay, seed reserves, fuel and supplies. Children/teens handle shared grazing in this village; no dedicated shepherd.
Weaving/spinning/sewing are distributed household activities, with HH-02 as a main craft anchor.
Miller combines milling, mechanism maintenance, carpentry/joinery and repairs. Smith handles ironwork, not only weapons.
No dedicated potter required; simple household making and externally acquired ceramics coexist.
Hunter/fisher supplements household resources and knows routes.
Jewish household's repair work and Tatar household's horse/service connection are selected individual biographies, not assignments to every member of a people.
Healer's knowledge/magic are individual training and fiction, not an inherited ethnic trait.

## Healer
Roma woman long known to neighbors, practical and observant, dry humor; healthcare, herbs and household ritual, rare real supernatural manifestations.
Ordinary house, small garden and work corner. Regional clothing with researched personal/cultural details; shared wool/sheepskin winter materials.
Husband does household and hired work; adult daughter has her own pursuits and helps partly; younger son joins local children/grazing.
Healer knows people/treatment; Vedun knows local spirits. They can exchange knowledge without one replacing the other.
No automatic fortune-teller costume, permanent caravan camp or magic stereotype.

## Starosta and authority conflict
Starosta is a local resident, no special bureaucratic uniform. Face, posture, behavior and better-kept outer garment can distinguish him.
May attempt negotiation at the spring, understanding consequences for neighbors. Exact scene and choice remain open.

## Initial displaced hub
Accepted initial group: eldest weaver daughter (ornament service), smith and healer.
These are recurring community NPCs, not a controllable companion party.
Departure of smith/healer relatives, exact reasons, timing and carrying logistics remain open.
Hunter assists escape but is not confirmed as a permanent hub member. Miller, starosta and most residents stay.
Portable kits, rain/cold layers, home/travel/temporary-hub variants reuse the same identities and base assets.
Smith leaves heavy equipment at home; later access to replacement/shared equipment needs narrative/gameplay resolution.
Letters/news/family ties make inaccessible home meaningful; no requirement that every family member needs a bespoke quest.
