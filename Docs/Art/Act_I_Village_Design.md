# Act I Home Village — Approved Design Brief
Status: v0.2, includes approved 23-household terrain layout, 2026-10-18.
Scope: home village, households, seasonal civilian art and first hero concepts. Written direction and terrain schematic approved; image selections and caveats are tracked in linked reviews.
Companions: [Community roster](../Vision/Act_I_Community.md), [character briefs](Act_I_Character_Briefs.md), [evidence dossier](../../References/Act_I_Evidence_Dossier.md), [sheet standard](Asset_Sheet_Standard.md).

## Evidence and approval
Approved game decisions are not automatically historical facts.
- Evidence: a scoped source supports the specific feature.
- Accepted extrapolation: user considers the interpretation sufficient for the game.
- Open detail: do not let a generator silently decide it.
Every child concept must link to evidence or inspiration, or state explicitly that it is original fiction with a reason. Do not substitute a generic reference homepage for an object-specific reference.
The project remains simple modular 3D: large forms, flat/soft shading, restricted palette, low surface noise, normal/mildly stylized proportions. No automatic mass generation.

## Village character and landscape
An ordinary, modest or slightly poorer village; no prosperous showcase settlement. It is a home worth returning to, with useful repairs and care rather than universal filth or pristine costumes.
Permanent homes stand on comparatively dry ground above wet meadow/marsh. Their yards, fields, pasture, forest access and water routes form a functional landscape.
Approved scale: exactly 23 households (nine named/reference households plus 14 additional local farms). This does not require 23 bespoke house models. [Terrain plan and estate assignments](Act_I_Village_Plan.md) supersede the earlier provisional range.
Most residents belong to local Belarusian/Ruthenian families. See roster for demographic caveats.
Home remains politically inaccessible after the conflict, not destroyed. Damage/curse sheets test reusable states and do not mandate burning the village.

Foundation geometry must suit terrain and support the structure, not float it on decorative stones.

## Farm buildings — B02/B06
Shared wall/roof vocabulary, differentiated by purpose:
- Granary/storehouse (klet/sviren): compact enclosed volume, firm door, no domestic-style windows; ventilation/supports to resolve.
- Livestock shed: low utilitarian volume, animal-appropriate access, few small openings; no domestic decoration.
- Threshing/harvest barn (gumno/klunya): wider working volume and larger doors; not merely a scaled-up house.
- Lean-to/work shelter: posts, roof, open side and optional rear wall.
- Earth-covered cellar: research candidate, not approved as a standard dwelling or finalized construction.
Basic yard may contain house + small storehouse + livestock shed. Smaller households may lack animal outbuildings; larger households may have several sheds. Do not attach every building to every home.
Dugouts/semi-dugouts are not the standard village housing type; cellar and temporary shelter must not be mislabeled as dwellings.

## Fences and gates — B03
Two functional families, not a universal fence around everything:
1. Light boundary: woven hazel or simple pole enclosure, according to selected yard use.
2. Horse enclosure: taller/stronger posts and horizontal poles or rough boards; selected braces on gates/sections.
Wattle: long hazel shoots with bark around stronger stakes; broad simplified weaving.
Pole/board family: pine poles and rough planks; partial bark removal and non-industrial regularity.
Diagonal bracing is accepted as a candidate construction detail; exact joint/fastening/date remains open. Avoid an unresearched repeating modern X-panel system.
R02 confirms several fence types in the wider estate corpus, not their frequency in poor local households. R12/R13 support material plausibility, not a proven XVI-century fence.
User explicitly accepted hazel/pine as sufficient game extrapolation.

## Ground and everyday dressing — B04/B05
Compacted ground at work areas and entrances, grass at edges, paths between actual destinations. Rain makes low points muddy/puddled without turning the whole yard into a swamp.
Optional small approach stones/short boards, modest woodpile, role-specific tools and limited useful containers. No decorative barrels/wheels/sacks placed everywhere.
Keep working/animal access open. Gates, doors and loading places determine paths.
Household water collection and the sacred spring are separate location functions; exact geographic relation remains open.

## Water access — B04/B05
- Shared sweep well (zhuravl) in a lower area with shallow water serves roughly 2–3 simple houses lacking animal outbuildings.
- Higher sites/deeper water favor a windlass well in our layout.
- Larger households with several livestock sheds have their own well, still accessible to neighbors.
These are selected game rules, not universal historical/hydrogeological laws: water depth ultimately determines mechanism.
Place wells on usable dry approaches away from polluted runoff; communal paths and a gathering area emerge around them.
Model both well mechanisms; share the well curb/base only where structurally plausible. R14 GAME record; detailed mechanisms to source.

## Workplaces
### Smithy
Small separate building toward the settlement edge with convenient access. Hearth, bellows, anvil on timber base, small hammer/tongs set, working surface and compact fuel/metal storage.
Do not assume a large modern double-horn anvil, fantasy chimney or random furnace. Hearth, bellows, exhaust, roof and fire arrangements need coherent sourced construction.
Approved location/function, not yet a modeling-ready building.

### Mill and woodworking shelter
Small watermill at a natural stream drop, without a large millpond.
Stable bank, short water feed if required, unobstructed downstream return, dry grain access; woodworking shelter on nearby drier ground.
Wheel type, head/flow, transmission and millstones remain open. Diagram water -> wheel -> transmission -> stones before modeling; no decorative nonfunctional wheel.
Miller is also the village carpenter/joiner; one character, two work setups. This is this village's biography, not a rule about all historical millers.

### Household crafts
Spinning, weaving, sewing, repair and preparation of supplies occur across households. Weaving family has a substantial shared work context.
No dedicated shepherd or potter NPC. Children/teens help with grazing and other age-appropriate tasks.
Simple homemade ceramic work is an accepted possibility; acquired/exchanged pottery remains part of supplies. Do not infer that all fine/wheel-thrown pottery is made by every family. Pottery wheel/kiln is not mandatory production scope.
Leather/shoe repair household is distinct from a full tanning works; no automatic tannery.

## Self-sufficiency
Cover food, seed/grain storage, hay, firewood, seasonal preservation, clothing and routine repairs. Some salt, metal and other supplies arrive through exchange.
These are content needs, not a commitment to a full economy/settlement simulation.

## Open before modeling
Metric dimensions; exact opening/shutter/hinge forms; smoke route; window membranes' mounting; roofs/joins; fence joints; well mechanisms; smithy equipment; watermill engineering; dated containers/tools.
Approved written choices permit bounded concept preparation; they do not mark historical research or image selection complete.
