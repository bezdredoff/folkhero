# Moodboard 01 — Ordinary People and Village

Status: first detailed visual-development board. This board defines the human baseline for the whole game.

## Why first

Heroes, soldiers, occult specialists and supernatural beings all need a normal world to contrast against.

The starting village/community should establish:
- what an ordinary person looks like;
- what materials they can afford;
- how clothing layers work;
- how houses and workspaces are assembled;
- what is clean, repaired, worn or valuable;
- how much ornament appears in daily life.

## Character groups to collect

- adult male farmer/laborer;
- adult female farmer/household worker;
- craftsperson;
- fisher/hunter/forest worker;
- older villager;
- merchant or itinerant trader;
- child/teen reference only where useful for settlement life;
- village head / comparatively prosperous household;
- displaced version of the same people with travel bundles and reused belongings.

## Clothing questions

For every candidate reference record:
- date;
- region;
- social class;
- garment name if known;
- fabric/material;
- fastening;
- footwear;
- headwear;
- seasonal layer;
- whether the element is primary-period evidence or later ethnographic continuity.

Do not approve a garment because it merely 'looks Slavic'.

## Everyday palette

Most daily clothing:
- natural linen whites/off-whites;
- undyed or muted wool;
- brown/gray;
- restrained plant-dye colors;
- weathered leather.

Red/white ornament:
- visible enough to become a Folkhero signature;
- concentrated on plausible textile zones;
- not every villager needs ceremonial-density embroidery;
- ceremonial/festive pieces should remain visually distinct from labor clothing.

## Village architecture

Prioritize:
- log/timber houses;
- thatch/wood-shingle roofing;
- fenced household compounds;
- barns;
- sheds;
- granaries;
- animal enclosures;
- wells;
- simple bridges;
- boat/fishing infrastructure;
- woodpiles;
- ovens/hearth-related structures;
- paths shaped by use rather than decorative planning.

Architecture references from later open-air museums may be used for construction vocabulary only when tagged with chronology caveats.

## Landscape integration

The village should feel embedded in the Upper Berezina ecology **without being built directly in the bog**.

Default spatial relationship:
- village / khutor on dry raised ground, a gentle hill, terrace or ridge;
- forest edge or mixed woodland close to the settlement;
- household gardens, yards and small fields around the buildings;
- lower ground used for meadow, haymaking and seasonal pasture;
- horses, cattle and sheep may occupy the low meadow zone;
- wet meadow and floodplain beyond;
- bog/marsh edge, reeds, sedge, open water and peatland farther out;
- river/spring access where appropriate;
- muddy seasonal roads may cross the low ground, but houses should not casually stand in permanent waterlogged soil;
- winter snow and spring flood variants should visibly alter the lowland zone before threatening the elevated village.

A useful visual-section rule is:

**settlement on rise -> fields/yards -> hay meadow/pasture -> wet meadow -> marsh/bog/water.**

This separation is important both for geographic believability and for gameplay readability: the player can understand when they are leaving human-managed land and entering liminal/supernatural wetland.

## Religion

In the starting rural board:
- Christianity is present but visually subordinate;
- a small cross, icon or local church may exist;
- avoid making church architecture the dominant village silhouette;
- Polotsk will carry a much stronger Christian/institutional layer later.

## Dirt and wear

Avoid both extremes:
- costume-drama pristine;
- universal filth.

Use:
- repaired clothes;
- worn hems;
- patched work garments;
- mud at boots/lower layers;
- soot around work;
- rain-darkened wood;
- carefully maintained valuable textiles.

## Generation brief seed

When generating exploratory concepts:
- show groups, not a single heroic model;
- include at least one work activity;
- include ordinary built environment;
- place permanent houses on visibly drier/elevated ground;
- show the marsh as adjacent lower terrain, with meadow/hayfield/pasture between village and true bog;
- keep magic absent in the first pass;
- use realistic proportions;
- use late-16th-century regional material culture as the design anchor;
- dark-fairytale atmosphere comes from weather, light and environment rather than fantasy props.

## Acceptance test

The board is ready for approval when we can answer:
- what does a poor villager wear in summer?
- what changes in cold/rain?
- what does a relatively prosperous villager wear?
- what textile/ornament level is everyday vs ceremonial?
- what is the standard house/farmstead silhouette?
- which details are evidenced and which are deliberate game extrapolation?
