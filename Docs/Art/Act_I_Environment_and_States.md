# Act I Environment, Creatures and Visual States
Status: v0.1 — accepted written art direction, discussion 2026-10-18 after village/character briefs.
Scope: visual design, not combat mechanics. Companion: [production pilot](Concept_Production_Pilot.md), [dossier](../../References/Act_I_Evidence_Dossier.md).
No image approval is implied by acceptance of this document.

## Ecology and asset scope
Use modern Upper Berezina / Berezinsky Reserve ecology as habitat evidence, not an exact XVI-century landscape reconstruction.
Approved full-act vocabulary:
- Scots pine, Norway spruce, black alder, silver birch and downy birch.
- Hazel understorey and willow shrubs where appropriate.
- Dry woodland, mixed forest on mineral islands, wet alder woodland, open sphagnum bog are distinct assemblies.
Black alder is a major wetland tree, not an occasional decorative addition.
Silver/downy birch are distinct; forest pine and stunted bog pine may need related but different geometry, not simple scale changes.
Oak/ash/aspen are research-supported contextual options, not all required in the pilot.
Vegetation uses a few canopy masses and clear trunk/branch silhouettes, not individual leaf detail.

## Lower vegetation and wetlands
Two distinct visual sets:
1. Open bog: broad sphagnum hummocks/depressions, scattered bog pine, sparse cotton-grass accents and low dwarf-shrub patches.
2. Lowland wetland/alder woodland: alder/downy birch, sedge, fern patches, willow where appropriate, water between ground masses.
Reeds are a separate bank habitat candidate; do not fill every bog with reeds.
Sphagnum is primarily surface/material plus broad hummock geometry; sedge uses simple curved tufts, fern a few broad stylized fronds, cotton-grass sparse light heads.
Specific species/association placement follows ecology references R19–R22.

## Deliberately reduced first route
Forest path -> wet alder woodland -> stream.
Initial route kit: spruce, black alder, ONE birch (species to select for actual patch), sedge and one low vegetation module.
Full pine forest/open sphagnum bog, extra plants, all age classes and all habitats are later expansion.
The production pilot below is even smaller: test one representative object per domain before building the route.

## Crossings
Start with ONE simple short timber bridge and ONE ford.
Bridge: reusable supports and deck sections; actual span/structure must remain plausible, not infinite procedural stretching.
Ford: shallow stream bed and gentle approaches, no separate building family.
Corduroy road/gat, narrow boardwalk and fallen-trunk crossing deferred.
Main grain/livestock route needs appropriate width/stability; narrow footpaths are separate uses.
Surface categories: solid ground, shallow water, sticky ground and blocked bog. Distinguish readable traversal intent; exact gameplay rules not locked here.

## Ritual spring and broken pact
Dominant landmark: ONE large natural boulder, water emerges near its base, worn approach and usable nearby clearing.
No separate ritual post; no automatic glowing runes or carvings. One simple offering vessel candidate, historically selected shape pending.
User-selected destruction: boulder is substantially fragmented; authority permanently removes some fragments. NOT a simple two-piece crack, not fully restorable by reassembling what remains.
Three location states: intact; freshly shattered/incomplete; aftermath of broken pact.
Keep recognizable large fragments plus grouped smaller rubble; avoid hundreds of individually modeled chips.
Show missing mass in comparison; exact fraction/shape, fragmentation method and removal motive remain open. Possible military-program connection is not a locked explanation.
Reuse the site layout, water and vegetation. Remaining debris matches original rock volume except for explicitly removed pieces.

## Bolotnik — visual only
Humanoid-compatible anatomy and standard humanoid rig are the overriding production constraints:
- human arrangement of head/shoulders/pelvis, full arms/legs, human hands and bare feet;
- broad heavy torso and substantial belly without extreme limb distortion;
- mild stoop through pose, not an incompatible skeleton;
- human-scale head, broad nose, heavy cheeks, simple mouth, eyes hidden/smoothed over;
- short clumped wet hair and small tangled beard, simple masses without separate strand animation.
Clothing: plain local shirt, trousers and unobtrusive belt, dark with water/soil, a few large wear/tear shapes; visible collar/cuffs/hem.
Barefoot explicitly approved; no shoes. Peat/mud skin and a few broad moss patches on body/clothes, not dense vegetation.
Suggested height 2–2.2 m remains a proposal, not an explicitly finalized measurement. Show 1.80 m human ruler.
Palette: shirt somewhat lighter than peat skin, trousers darker, muted green moss. Restrained wet highlights, flat/soft shading, low noise.
Same model/rig for calm/agitated states; pose/material/effects may differ.
Clothing is original adaptation; does not establish former-human origin. R08 folklore supports selected features, not pact neutrality or this costume.
Do not lock attacks, combat scale/balance or animation list: user explicitly returned discussion to art.

## Authority military family
Well-supplied visiting troops. Primarily cloth silhouettes, not armored fantasy villains.
- dense dyed wool cloth, maintained garments, practical leather footwear/belts;
- compact cloth headwear, minimal clothing metal; weapons/necessary fittings remain visible;
- baseline upper garment around mid-thigh; exact historical cut/side/date unresolved;
- senior figure uses same foundation with different headwear/cut and higher-quality finish.
Palette approved: gray-blue wool, undyed linen, dark brown leather, restrained muted red accent.
This is an artistic palette, not a documented uniform. Repeat compatible modules across many future soldiers; identity comes from consistent equipment/silhouette with variation, not identical modern uniforms.
Warm/cold/weather/wear variants reuse base and modular outer layers. Distinct units later vary weapons, optional protection and sourced insignia.
Two first references: ordinary soldier and senior. No extra combat-role roster mandated here.
Transport cart/packing for removed fragments is a candidate, not a fully selected construction.

## Ordinary water
One shared material family: tint, transparency, depth and flow parameters.
Spring/stream: bottom visible in shallow sections, sparse highlights and movement.
Peat water: brown/dark coloration is natural, not automatic poison/curse.
Deep areas: obscured bottom, bank/immersed objects communicate depth.
Flooded path: recognizable submerged terrain/grass/deck where appropriate.
Quiet water baseline; local motion/foam at mill drop, not foam everywhere.
Material feasibility/performance must be proved in URP; concept does not promise literal single-shader implementation without profiling.

## Status language
| State | Shape/material cue | Accent |
|---|---|---|
| Wet | Darkening, limited highlights/drops | No obligatory blue repaint |
| Frozen | Edge frost and limited ice volume | Pale blue |
| Burning | Flames/embers and char | Orange |
| Shock | Brief branched arcs | Pale yellow/white |
| Poison | Local film/deposit and sparse particles | Yellow-green |
| Curse | Broad sparse patches/veins | Plum/inky violet |
Color supplements shape. Winter frost needs edges/volume; poison must remain distinct among vegetation.
First cursed human: existing civilian body/rig/clothes, selected skin/clothing violet patches, changed posture/expression, rare glow. No new horns/limbs/costume.

## Curse across seasons
Preserve seasonal environment colors; no universal gray/cold-green wash.
Persistent: subdued plum/inky-violet patches, occasional broad veins/deposit, no dense runic noise.
Active event: brief lilac light. Reserve purple primarily for Curse, not every magic/poison state.
Summer: violet against green; autumn: darker plum against ochre/brown; winter: dark traces on snow/ice; wet/dark scenes may need restrained lighter edging.
Same cue shapes, background-dependent value/intensity. A pale glow alone must not be the sole winter cue.
Site/trees/terrain retain identity. Geometry corruption only where an actual silhouette change is needed.

## Seasons
Summer: green foliage/grass, exposed ground.
Autumn: ochre/brown leaf changes and selective leaf fall; conifers remain recognizable.
Winter: removable deciduous foliage, snow on eligible surfaces, selected ice. Not every water body automatically freezes.
Spring: wet ground, remnant snow, new growth and local flooding; full flood work deferred.
First detailed proof: summer + winter. Autumn initially palette/wetness with applicable foliage handling; full spring flood later.
Material, removable foliage/snow and genuine geometry change must be labeled separately.
